The runs follow L = E + A / N^alpha with E = 1.8, the entropy floor of the data. In the first exercise the vertical axis is the reducible loss L - E, so the law is a straight line on log axes and the ruler extrapolates two decades without error. A training step holds the weights, their gradients, the optimizer's two moments, and the activations of the batch in flight. Each column is one device. Per parameter, a plain fp32 step keeps 4 bytes of weight, 4 of gradient and 8 of Adam moments: 16 bytes, so a 1.3 B model is 21 GB before a single activation. Mixed precision moves the arithmetic to 2 bytes but keeps the 4-byte master copy and the 8-byte moments, so it saves activation memory and time, little else, slide 7. Gradient accumulation buys batch size with wall-clock time and no memory. Data parallel multiplies the batch, and only sharding the states (ZeRO, FSDP) divides the per-device memory. The target here is an effective batch of 256 on the card you have.

A patch of p by p pixels in three bands is flattened to 3 p squared numbers and projected to the model width; that vector plus a position tag is the token, slide 8. Nothing in the attention block knows the patches came from a grid: the shuffled image is the model's view without the tags. Smaller patches give a longer sequence and a finer grid, at quadratic cost in the score matrix; larger patches are cheap and coarse. The convolution of Week 4 had the grid built in, which is why it still wins on small labelled sets.

The reach test: compose a sparse pattern that still reaches everything

Forty-eight positions in a record. Full attention lets each one read all 48 at once. Compose a cheaper pattern from a local window, a stride and a few global tokens, click a source position, and watch how many hops its information needs to reach every other position. The challenge: everything within two hops, at most twelve entries per row.

A pattern is a fixed set of pairs that will be scored, chosen before training, slide 10. A window alone needs T / w hops to cross the record, which is the long reach attention was built for, thrown away. A stride adds a coarse grid, so distant positions meet in two hops. A global token is a shared summary that everyone reads and writes, and it collapses the diameter to two hops at a cost of one entry per row. The pattern is a prior about which pairs matter; the record's physics, not a paper about text, should pick it.

The step sequencer: tap an input into a state space layer and watch what it keeps

A two-state linear system, a damped oscillator, discretized with a zero order hold. Click steps on the input row to place impulses. The state evolves one step at a time and the output traces below; the kernel row shows the same layer as a convolution, the weight it gives to each past step. The continuous system is x' = A x + B u with A = [[-lambda, omega], [-omega, -lambda]], omega = 0.3, and y = C x. Holding u constant over a step of length Delta gives the recurrence x_k = A_bar x_(k-1) + B_bar u_k with A_bar = exp(A Delta), slide 15, the Kalman prediction step with learned matrices, slide 16. Unrolled, y is the convolution of u with the kernel K_k = C A_bar^k B_bar, slide 19. The half-life in steps is ln 2 / (lambda Delta): the step size and the decay set the timescale together, and a learned layer sets both from the data.

The distractor test: a fixed layer against a selective one

A record holds one cue early on and a burst of distractors somewhere after it. Two state space layers read the record: one with fixed matrices, one whose step size depends on the input. Drag the burst along the record and change its length, then read how much of the cue each layer still holds at the end.

Both layers run h_k = a_k h_(k-1) + (1 - a_k) u_k with a_k = exp(-Delta_k). The fixed layer uses one Delta for every step, so every distractor both decays the cue and writes itself in. The selective layer, slide 18, computes Delta from the input: large for a step it recognizes as a cue, near zero for a step it treats as noise, so the state holds through the burst. That is the gate of Week 5 in a cleaner form, and the reason the layer no longer has a single convolutional kernel: the kernel now depends on the record.
