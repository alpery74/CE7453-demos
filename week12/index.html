On the right is the batch similarity matrix from slide 6, rows for photos and columns for captions, with the true pairs on the diagonal. Each row is scored as a classification over the N captions and each column as a classification over the N photos; the loss sums the cross entropy of both. Its gradient pulls each photo toward its caption and pushes it away from the other N - 1.

Lower the temperature and the softmax sharpens, so near misses are punished harder and training moves faster until it becomes unstable. Raise N and every photo has more negatives to separate from, which is why real training uses batches of tens of thousands. In two dimensions the towers run out of room past about twelve pairs, which is the toy's limit.

Retrieval and zero-shot recognition are the same dot product; here it ranks photos against one phrase. With no rewording the query sits where the training captions put it and the three nearest photos are the right concept. The model orders the queue and makes no finding. Scores for relevant photos are drawn from N(d', 1) and the rest from N(0, 1), so d' is the only thing the model contributes. At d' = 1.2 reviewing the top fifth surfaces about sixty percent of what matters, the figure on slide 10, and the right panel shows the two score distributions with the cut.

Two numbers belong in the report together, the recall and the budget it was measured at. Move the budget to 100 percent and recall is 1 at any d', which is why a recall needs its operating point before it counts as a result.

Here the learned model is the state space form from slide 17 with the transition fitted from data, a true matrix plus a fixed random error of the chosen size. Inside that model the planner solves a least-squares problem for H steps of control, executes k of them on the real system, observes, and replans. With k equal to H it trusts its imagination to the end and the error compounds, as slide 18 says.

Set the model error to 0.2 and compare k = 1 with k = H. Replanning every step reaches the target with the same wrong model, because each observation resets the drift. That is why filters run recursively, and why a long horizon buys less than it promises.

A drift index is the standardized shift of the input distribution from its deployment baseline, computable from the inputs alone every month. Error is the held-out evaluation rerun on a schedule. It responds to drift quadratically here, as a model does when its inputs move away from the training data, so it stays inside the acceptance band for months after the inputs have left theirs.

Both thresholds were written into the validation report before deployment, and the readout is the lead time one chart gives over the other. Logging inputs earns that lead time, which is the whole of slide 20 in one number.
