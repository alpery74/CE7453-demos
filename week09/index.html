The picture at step t is x_t = sqrt(abar_t) x_0 + sqrt(1 - abar_t) eps, the closed form on slide 4, with one fixed noise draw per round. The schedule beta_t rises linearly from 0.0001 to 0.02 over T = 1000 steps, so abar_t falls below one half near t = 260 and below 0.02 by t = 600. Past that point every step looks the same, which is why the eye judges the signal-to-noise ratio. After each round the guess and the truth are added as dots on the abar curve, so a set of five rounds leaves a record of where the eye is reliable.

The training objective on slide 6 scores the network on the noise itself, L = E || eps - eps_theta(x_t, t) ||^2. The denoiser on the right has only x_t and a Gaussian prior over smooth 8 by 8 patches (the cosine-basis prior of the Spend the budget tab, scaled to 0.24 per pixel), and its estimate is the exact posterior mean of eps, the function a well-trained network converges to.     Guess the guidance weight from the samples

    A model has learned three land-cover clusters in a two-band reflectance space. It is asked for one class, roof, and sampled with classifier-free guidance at weight w.     Every sample follows eps_hat = eps_u + w (eps_c - eps_u), the line on slide 9, through 1000 ancestral steps, with exact noise predictions for the two Gaussian mixtures so that nothing is trained. In classifier-free guidance the unconditional model is the marginal of the conditionals, so the roof component of the three-class mixture is the requested class itself. w = 0 draws from that marginal, w = 1 is plain conditional sampling, and w above one draws from the tilted density p(x|c)^w p(x)^(1 - w), which has its own mean and its own width. The readout reports both: the spread relative to the class, and how far the cloud centre has moved from the class mean, in class widths. The roof and vegetation classes overlap here, which is what gives guidance something to suppress: raising w cuts the vegetation side of the roof cloud, so the width falls from about 0.93 of the class at w = 1 to about 0.85, and the centre walks away from the class mean, reaching about one class width at w = 10. Sharpening and displacement are the same operation seen from two sides. Where two classes are far apart the ratio p(x|c) / p(x) is nearly flat across the class, guidance has nothing to cut, and the width holds while the samples still move. Both predictions are needed per step, so guidance doubles the cost of every sample.

    Three gauges plus an equation: fit a river profile

    A 100 m reach has a hidden water-surface profile h(x) that obeys d2h/dx2 = s(x) with a known, smooth source. Drag the three gauges to where you want readings, commit to which fit will be closer, and compare a fit from the readings alone with a fit from the readings and the equation.

    Both fits use 24 Gaussian radial basis functions plus a linear term. The data-only fit is a ridge least-squares solution through the three readings, each with 2 cm of noise. The second fit adds the residual of d2h/dx2 = s(x) at 30 collocation points with a fixed weight lambda = 1, the loss L_data + lambda L_pde on slide 15. The source panel is drawn in units of 1e-4 per metre, the scale d2h/dx2 takes with h and x in metres: the curvature of the hidden profile peaks at about 3.4e-3 per metre. In round 2 the source handed to the fit is 20 % too large while the hidden profile stays the same, so the equation fit carries a model error. The Reveal the map tab turns lambda into the quantity to choose once s(x) cannot be trusted.

    Each fit minimizes L = L_data + lambda L_pde + w_bc L_bc, slide 14, over 40 radial basis functions with w_bc fixed and the PDE residual normalized by the size of the source. Small lambda ignores the equation and interpolates three noisy points; large lambda ignores the gauges and solves an equation whose source is wrong. The beam panel carries the same quantity as the error figures in the map: the true field runs from -0.12 to 1.62 in temperature rise, so an error of 0.065 is about 4 % of its range. The valley between them moves as the source error grows, which is the stochastic model decision on slide 16.
