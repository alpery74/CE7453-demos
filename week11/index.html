Slide 9 makes this argument after Schaeffer, Miranda and Koyejo (2023), and the homework asks for the same plot. Whether every reported emergent ability is of this kind is still argued. Any threshold metric does this, so measure on your task with a metric that moves continuously.

Mechanically this is Bayesian regression. Pretrained weights encode a prior over rules, a ~ N(1.0, 0.3^2) and b ~ N(0, 0.5^2), and the examples in the prompt condition that prior. On the right the belief over (a, b) tightens around the true rule as k grows, with no gradient step anywhere.

At k = 0 the model answers from the prior, which is where confident wrong answers come from. By four examples the rule is unambiguous on the page and the prediction sits on it. What you paste in is the tool; that is the point of slide 12.

A rank-r adapter can at best keep the r largest singular values of Delta W and drop the rest, so its error is the energy in the tail. It trains 2 d r parameters where the full matrix has d^2: at d = 64 and r = 8 that is 1024 against 4096, and at the widths of a real model the ratio is hundreds to one.

Drag the concentration slider toward 0.98 and the update needed by the task spreads across many directions. Rank 8 then keeps a small share, and the adapter learns less than a full fine tune would. That is the restriction on slide 16, and it is the same restriction that protects what the base already knew.

On the left the whole trade sits in one curve, with every threshold a point on it, and the readouts are the two numbers slide 19 asks you to report together. A Wilson interval on the answered items widens as the model refuses more, because fewer items remain to score it on.

Switch to the overconfident model and the right panel shows the gap slide 20 describes: stated confidence sits above observed accuracy in every bin. Abstaining still helps, because the ranking of items by confidence is still informative, but the threshold you set from the stated numbers no longer means what it says. Calibrate first, then set the threshold in advance.
