The leaking pipelines carry the classics on slide 5: a scaler fit on all rows before the split, so the test rows' mean and spread are inside the training features; near-duplicate samples, here the same culvert inspected in consecutive years, landing on both sides of the split; and a threshold chosen by watching the test score, which turns the test set into a second training set. With two percent cracks, the detector that never flags anything scores 98 percent accuracy and finds nothing, which is slide 9. The expected cost per weld is p(miss) times its price plus p(false alarm) times its price; minimizing it puts the threshold on the posterior at c_FA / (c_FA + c_miss), which is 0.005 at the one-to-two-hundred ratio on slide 10. The grey marker at 0.5 shows what the default would have cost on the same welds. The ROC curve barely moves as the threshold crosses the rare class; the precision-recall curve shows the struggle, which is why it is the picture to use under imbalance.

Beat the free predictor: forecast a river stage from any day you choose

Two hundred days of river stage at a gauge, in a stormy record or a calm one. Pick the column you expect to win, then click any day to make it the forecast origin: a flexible model fitted to the thirty days before it, and a sober regression on the last five days' values, forecast a week ahead against the two predictors that cost nothing, persistence and the long-run mean. The next seven days are then revealed and the tally records who won. Click three or more origins and read the verdict.

the record

the tally over your origins

The flexible model is a cubic in time fitted to the last thirty days, the kind of trend extrapolation that looks skilled in a scatter and fails forward; the lag-5 model is a linear regression on the five previous days, refitted on every day before the origin and rolled forward. Persistence says tomorrow equals today, the mean says every day equals the record's average. Skill is one minus the model's mean squared error over the free predictor's; a negative skill means the model would have been worse than doing nothing. In the stormy record jumps arrive without warning and persistence is unbeatable, as slide 11 warns; in the calm record a smooth season gives the lag-5 regression something real to learn.

The probability of transit is sigmoid(w . x + b): the solid line is where it equals one half, the dashed lines are where it equals 0.9 and 0.1, and the distance between them sets the steepness. Fitting by maximum likelihood minimizes the Bernoulli cross entropy, the loss Week 1 promised, and its gradient is residual times feature, the least-squares shape on slide 15. The ratio of the two weights is a rate of exchange between minutes and dollars, which is what a transport planner reads off a logit model. Weighting the two errors by their costs moves the decision line to the posterior c_FA / (c_FA + c_miss), slide 14, and the line itself does not move.

There is no training step: the boreholes are the model and distance does all the work, so the coordinates must be in the same units. One neighbour reproduces every borehole, misread logs included, and scores 100 percent on the data it memorized while the map turns to patchwork; a large committee votes the misreads down and smooths the boundary, and at k equal to the sample size the whole site becomes the majority class. The kernel is the same idea with the hard cutoff replaced by a weight that fades with distance, so the prediction stops jumping as boreholes enter and leave the committee, and the bandwidth is the same bias and variance knob as k. Inverse distance weighting and kriging are this picture in geodesy.

Among all separating lines the support vector machine keeps the one with the widest gap to the nearest point on each side, slide 21; only those nearest points, the support vectors, hold the street up, and every other section could be deleted without moving it. A margin is a robustness statement: it says how far the boundary could sit from where it does given the sample in hand. When the classes overlap, the soft margin trades width against violations through one constant C, hinge loss plus a squared norm penalty in Week 1's shape.

Lift the ring: the kernel trick with the lift written out

Sensor readings where the faults sit inside a ring of normal readings. No straight line in the plane separates them. Add a third coordinate, height equal to distance from the centre squared, and the readings lift onto a bowl where a flat slice at one height does separate them. Type the height you would slice at, lock it in, and the readings lift; the slice's shadow on the plane is a circle. Then drag the slice.

The ring is slide 22: in the plane no line separates the classes, but with one added coordinate, distance from the centre squared, a flat slice does, and the slice's height is a circle back in the plane. The support vector machine only ever needs inner products between lifted points, so a kernel function returns those products without building the lift; the radial basis kernel corresponds to an infinite-dimensional lift and costs one distance evaluation. Curved boundaries come from straight ones in a higher space.
