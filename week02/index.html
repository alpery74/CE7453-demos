The three leaks are the classics on slide 5: a scaler fit on all rows before the split, so the test rows' mean and spread are inside the training features; near-duplicate samples, here the same bridge inspected in consecutive years, landing on both sides of the split; and a threshold chosen by watching the test score, which turns the test set into a second training set. The inflated scores are typical of what Kapoor and Narayanan found across published studies. None of the three authors meant to cheat.

The field is smooth over a few hundred metres, so a sample's nearest neighbour in a random split is usually a few metres away and carries nearly the same value; the random score is a test of memory, not of prediction. Painting whole blocks forces the model to predict where it has no near neighbours, which is the deployment question on slide 7, and a buffer ring stops the correlation reaching across the fence. The survey block in the middle was never sampled; its error is what a client would actually see on new ground, and a painted block of about the same size should land near it while the random score flatters.

With two percent cracks, the detector that never flags anything scores 98 percent accuracy and finds nothing, which is slide 9. The expected cost per weld is p(miss) times its price plus p(false alarm) times its price; minimizing it puts the threshold on the posterior at c_FA / (c_FA + c_miss), which is 0.005 at the one-to-two-hundred ratio on slide 10. The ROC curve barely moves as the threshold crosses the rare class; the precision-recall curve shows the struggle, which is why it is the picture to use under imbalance.

Beat the free predictor: forecast a river stage from any day you choose

Two hundred days of river stage at a gauge. Click any day to make it the forecast origin: a flexible model fitted to the thirty days before it, and a sober regression on the last five days' values, forecast a week ahead against the two predictors that cost nothing, persistence and the long-run mean. The next seven days are then revealed and the tally records who won. Click five or six origins and read the skill.

the tally over your origins

The flexible model is a cubic in time fitted to the last thirty days, the kind of trend extrapolation that looks skilled in a scatter and fails forward; the lag-5 model is a linear regression on the five previous days, refitted on every day before the origin and rolled forward. Persistence says tomorrow equals today, the mean says every day equals the record's average. Skill is one minus the model's mean squared error over the free predictor's; a negative skill means the model would have been worse than doing nothing, which under honest forward chaining is common on a series that is close to a random walk, as slide 11 warns.

Draw the choice boundary: three hundred commuters, car or transit

Each commuter is a point: how many minutes longer transit takes across, how many dollars cheaper it is up. Blue took transit, red drove. Drag the two handles to place a boundary line and drag the dashed line to set how sharply the probability ramps across it: that is logistic regression drawn by hand, the logit model of slide 15. The score is the cross entropy, and the reliability diagram on the right says whether your probabilities mean what they say.

The probability of transit is sigmoid(w . x + b): the line is where it equals one half, the dashed line is where it equals 0.9, and the distance between them sets the steepness. Fitting by maximum likelihood minimizes the Bernoulli cross entropy, the loss Week 1 promised, and its gradient is residual times feature, the least-squares shape on slide 15. The ratio of the two weights is a rate of exchange between minutes and dollars, which is what a transport planner reads off a logit model. The reliability diagram is slide 18: a line placed well but with too steep a ramp is overconfident, and the diagram bows away from the diagonal.

Among all separating lines the support vector machine keeps the one with the widest gap to the nearest point on each side, slide 21; only those nearest points, the support vectors, hold the street up, and every other section could be deleted without moving it. The ring is slide 22: in the plane no line separates the classes, but with one added coordinate, distance from the centre squared, a flat slice does, and the slice's height is a circle back in the plane.
